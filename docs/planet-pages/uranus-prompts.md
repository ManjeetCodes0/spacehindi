## 5. `bg03_uranus_methane_blue.webp` — Section 3: नीला रंग — मीथेन का जादू
**Context:** Methane in Uranus's atmosphere absorbs red light and reflects blue-green — why it looks cyan
> Beautiful cinematic light spectroscopy visualization showing how methane creates Uranus's color. Sunlight — shown as a full white-rainbow spectrum — enters Uranus's atmosphere from the top. As it descends through the methane-rich atmosphere, the red end of the spectrum is progressively absorbed by methane molecules shown as yellow molecular structures. The blue-green wavelengths pass through and reflect back up, giving Uranus its distinctive cyan color. Above Uranus, the reflected light exits as pure blue-green. The visualization is part scientific diagram, part visual poetry. The electromagnetic spectrum is visually beautiful. Color palette: full white/rainbow sunlight entering, methane yellow-orange absorption, cyan-green blue light reflecting upward, Uranus cyan planet, deep black space. 8K, scientific visualization quality.

> Dramatic cinematic view of Uranus's ring system from a ring-plane perspective. Unlike Saturn's brilliant icy rings, Uranus's 13 rings are nearly as dark as charcoal — composed of dark material with very low albedo. They appear vertical relative to the orbital plane due to Uranus's extreme tilt. The rings are thin and precise — dark arcs against the pale cyan of Uranus and the black of space. Backlit by a distant Sun, the rings barely catch light but are hauntingly visible as dark structures. This is the ring system that hid from us until 1977 — discovered only because they blocked a star during an occultation event. The mood is hidden mystery revealed. Backlit ring visualization, extreme darkness contrast. Color palette: charcoal dark rings barely visible, pale cyan Uranus disc, distant Sun backlight catching ring edges, deep black space, minimal color palette. 8K, dark and dramatic cinematic composition.

## 8. `bg06_uranus_seasons.webp` — Section 6: 42 साल का मौसम
**Context:** Each season on Uranus lasts 42 Earth years — poles get 42 years of continuous sunlight then 42 years of darkness
> Cinematic time-series visualization of Uranus's extreme seasons over its 84-year orbit. Four orbital positions of Uranus around the Sun show the dramatic seasonal changes: position 1 — north pole pointing directly at Sun in continuous summer daylight (sunlit pole bright); position 2 — equinox, rings visible from the solar equatorial plane; position 3 — south pole pointing toward Sun, north in 42-year darkness; position 4 — opposite equinox. At each position, Uranus's illuminated surface shows which pole is in summer/winter. The 98° tilt means one pole faces the Sun for decades while the other freezes in permanent night. Color palette: Uranus cyan, Sun brilliant gold, four orbital positions, sunlit/shadow hemispheres, orbital path gold line, deep black space. 8K, scientific orbital visualization cinematic quality.

The ice mantle background is the bizarre superionic water state — simultaneously solid lattice and flowing ions. This is a diamond mine inside a planet.
